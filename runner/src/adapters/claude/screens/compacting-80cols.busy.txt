● Herons are long-legged wading birds found on every continent except
  Antarctica, and they are among the most patient hunters in the animal kingdom.
  Standing motionless in shallow water, a heron can wait for minutes or even
  hours before striking with a lightning-fast jab of its dagger-like bill to
  snatch a fish, frog, or crustacean. Their necks are folded into a distinctive
  S-curve at rest and extend smoothly when they lunge, a feature that helps them
  reach prey while staying hidden in reeds and marsh grass. Many species, such
  as the great blue heron, are large, reaching nearly four feet tall with
  wingspans of six feet, yet they fly with slow, deliberate wingbeats, their
  necks tucked back against their shoulders. Herons are also remarkably
  adaptable, nesting in colonies high in trees or on cliffs and sometimes in
  mixed groups with egrets and ibises. Their presence often signals healthy
  wetlands, since they depend on clean water and abundant prey. Across cultures,
  herons have inspired myths and symbols of solitude, vigilance, and grace.
  Watching one stand perfectly still at the water's edge is a reminder that
  patience, when paired with precise timing, can be its own kind of power.
